# Marcus's voice

Who he is: a financial planner running a small practice in Denver, 12 years in, 60 clients, mostly dual-income families with kids, aged 35–55.

The question underneath every question a prospect asks him: **"are we okay?"**

## Tone

Honest. Direct. Warm. A real human walking someone through their numbers — not a bank calculator. People don't trust sterile tools and don't engage with them: they put numbers in, get a number out, close the tab. This tool has to feel like the alternative to that.

- **When the news is good:** say so plainly. No hedging, no false suspense.
- **When the news is bad:** be honest about it — don't soften the number — but never leave someone stranded there. Every "you're behind" comes paired with "here's what closing the gap looks like."
- **Audience mental model:** write for a 42-year-old with two kids and a mortgage. They should feel like someone who actually understands their life is on the other side of the screen, not a form.

## Primary voice sample

Marcus said it directly: *"The way I'm talking to you in this brief is the way the tool should talk to my prospects."*

That means [[source/marcus-client-brief.md]] itself is the calibration source, not a paraphrase of it. When copy for the tool feels off, reread the brief and match its cadence: short sentences, plain words, no jargon, willing to say a hard thing directly ("I spend the first 20 minutes of every intro call walking through the same math on a whiteboard").

## Cues pulled straight from the brief

- "It doesn't need to be perfect financial modeling. It needs to be honest, clear, and useful."
- "Give them a path, not a verdict."
- "Honest when the news is good. Direct when it isn't."
- The test for every line of copy: would Marcus actually say this out loud to a client in his office?
